# AI strategy: Designer

> Prioritized AI adoption map. **Date: 2026-06.** Sources: GDPval, AEI, O*NET, and

!!! abstract "Context"
    Visual generation is getting cheaper very quickly, and generic work is losing value. Taste,
    art direction, and the ability to solve a business problem are becoming more valuable.

## Where to start: the first round

The tables are a map of what is possible, not your plan. Start with
[process scoring](../../playbooks/process-scoring.md). Ask whether the work repeats, has enough
volume, produces a checkable result, carries an affordable error cost, and has usable data. For
most designers, the first round looks like this:

1. **No. 2, routine image work such as backgrounds, upscaling, and resizing.** It repeats often
   and has enough volume. You can check the result by eye in seconds, and an error costs a rework,
   not a publication.
2. **No. 1, concepts and mood boards.** This is research, not the final asset. A person chooses the
   direction immediately, so the error cost is close to zero while the work remains a sketch.
3. **No. 3, UI copy and icons.** The check is direct. An icon either communicates the action and
   follows the brand guide, or it does not.

**Not in the first round:** No. 4, "variations for audiences and channels," only makes sense after
production in No. 2 and selection in No. 1 are stable. Otherwise, you multiply variations of weak
material. No. 5, rights to AI-generated images, and No. 6, art direction and the brand system, are
permanent rules rather than items in an automation queue. Check rights before and after every
round. Art direction is where human judgment keeps its full weight.

## Keep these parts of design human

- **Taste and the final choice.** AI supplies options; a person selects one and owns that choice.
- **Rights to AI-generated images used commercially.** Complete the legal check before delivery.
- **The client relationship and defense of the idea.** A person explains and stands behind the decision.
- **The client's brand guides.** Treat them as confidential and do not send them to open services without permission.
- **Art direction and the brand system.** Separate generations cannot guarantee a coherent whole.

## Review points and stop thresholds

| Initiative | What a person checks | Stop threshold (example, replace with your own) |
|---|---|---|
| No. 2 routine image work | sample the output after automatic processing for artifacts, cropping, and color | a rejection rate above your threshold means returning the batch to manual processing until the setup is fixed |
| No. 1 concepts and mood board | select a direction that fits the brief and the task | if none of the options fits, clarify the brief instead of forcing a choice |
| No. 3 UI copy and icons | compare the icon with the action description and brand guide | if users cannot understand the icon or it breaks the guide, revise it manually |

Before you expand the flow, build a [reference set of 20 cases](../../playbooks/eval-set-builder.md):
20 accepted concepts or icons with known selection outcomes.
