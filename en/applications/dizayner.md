# Designer: what to automate and which tools to use

> Field: **Marketing and content**. Role hub. **Checked: 2026-08-18.** Tools change over time.
> Sources for this review are at the bottom of the page.

## At a glance

Visual generation is getting cheaper very quickly. Taste, a broad visual reference library, solving the
business problem, and client work become **more valuable**. The risks are an attractive image that misses
the goal and unclear rights to AI-generated images.

!!! info "What the data says (GDPval, Anthropic Economic Index, O*NET)"
    - **Capability (GDPval and market evidence):** image generation and routine work such as background
      removal, upscaling, and resizing are getting cheaper very quickly.
    - **Use (AEI):** automation is growing in the basic production layer of visual work.
    - **The 2026 data argues with the simple "GenAI killed design" conclusion.** The split runs inside
      the profession: employment growth for graphic designers is modest, around 2% through the
      mid-2030s, while UX roles are projected at about **+16%**. Design budgets are mostly holding or
      rising: 47% of businesses increased them, 32% kept them flat, and only 12% cut.
    - **The premium sits on design plus AI:** designers with AI skills earn roughly **56% more** than
      peers without them, and 69% of marketing and creative leaders say AI is reshaping the skills
      they need. What got cheaper is visual production, not design as a profession.
    - **Mode:** augment moving toward replacement. Generic work loses value. Taste, art direction, a design
      system, and solving the business problem remain. See the [full data review](_super-analysis.md) and
      the [role AI strategy](strategy/dizayner.md).

## What to automate first

1. **Concepts and mood boards.** Explore styles and directions quickly before detailed work.
2. **Image routine.** Remove backgrounds, upscale, resize, and adapt assets for different formats.
3. **Variations and iterations.** Generate many options quickly, then make the selection yourself.
4. **UI copy and icons.** Draft interface text and generate icons from a description with Figma plugins.
5. **Social content visuals.** Build templates and channel adaptations.

## Task review: what AI can do and what you must check

| Task (O*NET) | Give to AI: method or tool | Keep or verify yourself | Prompt to start |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Concepts and mood board | Generate directions | Select for the actual goal | `Give me five directions for this idea. I will choose` |
| Image routine | Background removal, upscaling, resizing | Result quality |  |
| Variations | Produce many versions quickly | Taste and final choice | `Give me five variations of this composition` |
| UI copy and icons | Draft text and create icons from a description | Brand fit | `Generate icons for these actions` |

> for every AI-generated image you plan to use.

## Tools by use case

=== "Leading assistants"

    - **ChatGPT or Claude** for ideas, copy, and briefs.

=== "Specialist tools"

    - **Midjourney**, **DALL-E**, and **Adobe Firefly** for generation and editing; **Figma AI** and
      plugins such as Magician for UI copy and icons; **Canva AI**; and **Looka** for branding.

## One practical playbook

!!! example "From brief to concepts in one hour"
    1. Write the brief, then generate five directions in Midjourney or Firefly for a mood board.
    2. Select one or two and refine the background, resolution, and composition in an editor.
    3. **Human check:** you own the fit with the business problem and the brand voice.
    4. Check whether the rights for each AI-generated image allow commercial use.
    5. Output: the research takes an hour. Taste and the decision remain yours.

## Where not to use AI

!!! danger "Red flags"
    - **Copyright and other rights for AI images:** check the service terms and whether commercial use is allowed.
    - **Final taste and the design system:** AI gives options. A person selects them.
    - **A client's brand guidelines:** do not upload them to an open service without permission.

## Prompt patterns: weak and better

| Weak | Better |
| --- | --- |
| `Make a logo` | `Give me five directions for this idea. I will choose and refine one` |
| `Make it look good` | `Give me three solutions to this problem and explain the risk in each one` |

## Where to move your effort

Move time from production into art direction, the design system, the business problem, and defending the
idea with the client.

### The levels ladder in this role

The five levels from Chapter 5, in the language of this profession. Mark where you stand in your main
tasks this week.

| Level | What it looks like here |
|---|---|
| **1. AI user** | I generate visuals and clear the routine work: backgrounds, upscaling, resizing |
| **2. Validator** | I select and finish. Generic output can be beautiful and still miss the brief; telling those apart is my job |
| **3. Orchestrator** | I built the flow from brief to options to selection to finish, with AI supplying the volume of options |
| **4. Outcome owner** | I own whether the design actually solves the business problem, not how many mockups I made |
| **5. System builder** | I built the design system and rules that produce the right result without me |

**Where people usually get stuck.** Level 1 is where people stop: plenty of options, no taste being trained. Art direction and defending an idea to the client are what gain value.
