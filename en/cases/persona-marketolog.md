# Example: Marina, a marketer, completes the full Volume 1 workbook

> Marina is a composite character, and the figures are illustrative. This example shows what honest
> answers can look like in the [Volume 1 workbook](../playbooks/tom1-workbook.md).

**Marina is 34 and works in marketing at a B2B services company with about 80 employees.** She handles
content, newsletters, landing pages, and lead reporting. She uses AI "like everyone else": to draft a
post or rewrite an email. Her concern is familiar: "The machine writes the drafts now. What is left for me?"

---

## Step 1. Value lever, Chapter 2

- **The main lever in my work:** attention and trust, lever ③. The company wins business because
  prospects trust us before the first call.
- **What gets cheaper, the output:** post copy, banners, and newsletter drafts. Anyone can produce these now.
- **What remains mine, the outcome:** the result I am accountable for is leads from the "right"
  customers and a reputation that makes people recommend us.

*Why this answer is honest:* Marina did not write, "My lever is content." Content is output. A lever is
what someone pays for.

## Step 2. Sort the week into baskets, Chapter 3

She listed 13 tasks from the previous week using her calendar. The result:

- **Share of time in 🟢 "AI alone":** about 35%, including post drafts, newsletter excerpts, an open-rate
  report, and webinar descriptions.
- **The main 🔴 task where I cannot be replaced:** talking with sales about which leads are right for us
  and which are noise, then changing the positioning based on that conversation.
- An uncomfortable finding: 3 of the 13 tasks are "busy work" with no value lever. One of them is
  moving the same report from one spreadsheet to another.

## Step 3. The ladder, Chapter 4

- **Entry / middle / top:** the middle. People assign tasks to me, but I have no one to delegate to.
- **One nearby foothold:** the contractor budget for design goes through me. That is already a small
  piece of ownership, and I can expand it.

## Step 4. Usefulness level and a +1 move, Chapter 5

- **My default level:** 2, "I do what I am asked, do it well, and use AI to do it faster."
- **Candidate task for +1:** the monthly lead report. Today I "make" it. I want to own the conclusion.
  The deliverable should be a page called "What this means and what we change," with my name on it,
  rather than a spreadsheet.

## Step 5. Personal system, Chapter 6

- **My weakest layer:** verification. AI writes drafts, but I do not have a checklist for accepting them.
  I rely on feel, which makes me afraid to delegate more.
- **What AI already does in my tasks:** drafts, headline options, and call summaries.

## Step 6. One skill that is gaining value, Chapter 7

- **The skill I train deliberately:** customer judgment. Once a week, I review two deals with the sales
  team. Why did the customer arrive? Why did they buy or leave? The data does not contain those answers.

## Step 7. My mode of working with AI, Chapter 8

- **My mode:** honestly, I am a self-automator when I work on copy. I delegate it and do not read deeply.
- **What I do without AI once a week:** I write one piece from scratch each month. It is a positioning
  piece where I must form the idea, not fill a format. This is training, not production.

## Step 8. Diagnosis, Chapter 9

- **What gets cheaper:** content production. About 35% of my week is already in 🟢.
- **Weak point:** I have no verification loop of my own and accept AI work by feel.
- **Value that does not burn away:** knowledge of the customer, trust from sales, and a sense for whether
  a lead is right for us.
- **My +1:** own the conclusions about leads, not the spreadsheet.

## Step 9. Who I am, Chapter 10

- **I am responsible for:** bringing in the right customers and making sure they arrive prepared.
  My old answer was, "I create content and newsletters."

## Step 10. A 90-day plan, Chapter 11

- **One task:** the monthly lead report.
- **Old rule:** "I collect the figures in a spreadsheet and send it out."
- **New rule:** "AI collects the figures using my template → I check them against a 5-point checklist
  → I write half a page of conclusions → I defend them in the planning meeting."
- **The capability I keep:** interpretation. I explain why the figures look this way and what should change.
- **A verifiable change after 90 days:** the planning meeting discusses my conclusions rather than my
  spreadsheet. At least one decision each quarter follows my recommendation.
- **The next level of responsibility:** ownership of one channel's budget.
- **What remains as a system:** a report template, a verification checklist, and 30 minutes blocked in
  the calendar for conclusions.

---

## What to notice in this example

1. **None of the answers is heroic.** Marina does not become an "AI evangelist." She moves her effort
   from production, which is getting cheaper, to judgment and ownership of the conclusion, which are
   gaining value.
2. **The weak point was not where she expected it.** The issue is not "I cannot prompt." It is "I do
   not have my own verification loop." This is common because verification limits delegation.
3. **The plan rests on one task.** She is not trying to "change careers." She has one report, one habit,
   and one metric. That is a realistic scale for 90 days.
