# Skill: my usefulness level

> A zero-install tool for **Chapter 5**, "Five Levels of New Usefulness." It runs in your browser and
> sends no data anywhere. Code license: MIT.

This tool shows where one real task sits: operator, AI user, validator, orchestrator, outcome owner, or
system builder.

<a class="md-button md-button--primary" href="tool.html">Open the tool</a>

## What it does

- You choose one specific task from the week and mark its current level.
- You answer questions that push back against a flattering self-rating. The tool **will not let you
  claim a higher level** when the task has no definition of "correct," or when the solution cannot run
  without you.
- It returns an honest level and one specific **move to level up by one**.

## How to use it

1. Open the tool and review one or two important tasks from the week.
2. Apply the suggested one-level move during the next few weeks.
3. You can tell an AI assistant: "Here is my task and level. Help me define one move up and show what I
   should check." Ask for options, not a finished decision.

## Discipline

The level describes a task, not a person. You may work at different levels on different tasks. Do not
turn the result into a label. Raise the level where that change will create the most useful result,
not everywhere at once.

## Chat version if you cannot use the browser tool

Copy this prompt into any AI chat, then describe your task.

```text
Help me identify the honest usefulness level of my task and the one move that would raise it by one.
The levels, from low to high, are:
1. Operator: I do the work myself without AI.
2. AI user: I give the task to AI and use the draft as it is.
3. Validator: I check and correct the AI result against a clear definition of right and wrong.
4. Orchestrator: I manage several steps, tools, or agents toward one result.
5. Outcome owner: I own the full outcome, and AI and people are tools I use.
(Some versions add level 0, system builder: it runs repeatedly without my participation.)

Here is my task: [describe one specific task from your week]

Do not let me overstate the level. If the task has no clear definition of "correct," it is not level 3
yet. If the solution needs my constant participation, it is not a system. Name the real level and
suggest one specific move up by one level. Give me one move, not a five-step plan.
```
