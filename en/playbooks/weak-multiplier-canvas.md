## Where the Leverage multiplier comes from

The idea comes from investor and entrepreneur Naval Ravikant's 2018 thread, "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)," and Eric Jorgenson's 2020 book *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant*, available at navalmanack.com. Naval describes three forms of leverage:

- **Labor:** Hiring and delegating to other people.
- **Capital:** Money that can keep working.
- **Permissionless leverage:** Code and media. They can be copied at almost no cost, and you do not need someone's approval to use them, unlike labor and capital.

In this canvas, **transferability** is a practical test for the third form of leverage. It asks whether an asset can keep working and be copied without your constant involvement. Naval also names two pillars that do not appear as separate multipliers here but still matter:

- **Specific knowledge:** Knowledge that grew from your path and curiosity, rather than a textbook. It is hard to copy or replace with an identical hire.
- **Judgment:** When leverage such as AI is cheap and widely available, the amount of labor matters less than the quality of the decisions about what to do with it.

This is not a formula for wealth or a guarantee of results. It is one investor's view, not a research finding. This canvas borrows the structure without borrowing anyone else's numbers or promises.
