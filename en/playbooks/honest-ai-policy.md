# An honest AI policy: trust as part of the product

> Practicum for **Book 3, *The Economics of Trust*** (the Creation house). Time: 20 to 30 minutes.
> A one-page worksheet for an independent professional or small business. You will leave with a short statement you can show a client without embarrassment.

Clients increasingly want to know where you use AI, where a person stays involved, and who takes responsibility when the machine gets something wrong. Silence can look like concealment. An honest AI policy is not a legal document. It answers the client's questions before they have to ask. People pay a premium for honesty, not for a business that pretends to be either "fully human" or "fully AI."

---

## Part 1. Where we use AI and where a person takes over

Review your services and mark each one honestly:

| What we do | AI works on its own | AI works under human review | A person does the work |
|---|---|---|---|
| `______________` | | | |
| `______________` | | | |
| `______________` | | | |

Rule: **the higher the cost of an error for the client, the farther right you should move.** A person must stay responsible when the work affects money, health, legal rights, or an irreversible decision.

---

## Part 2. Who takes responsibility for an error?

Write one sentence a client can understand without a lawyer:

- The person responsible for the result is: `__________` (a name or role, not "the system").
- If AI makes an error, the client should do this, and we will do this: `__________________`
- The client can reach a person here, without fighting the bot: `__________________`

> Easy access to a person is a product feature, not overhead. It matters even more when every competitor sends clients to a bot.

---

## Part 3. Set a boundary: who are you not right for?

Trust grows when you say clearly **who you cannot help** and what you will not do. A boundary does not drive away the right clients. It helps them see that you are not promising everything to everyone.

- We **do not** take on: `__________________`
- We are not a good fit if the client needs: `__________________`
- We will not compromise these principles: `__________________`

> A boundary filters out poor fits and makes your offer easier to trust for the people you can help. "We are not for everyone" is more honest than "we serve everyone."

---

## Part 4. Put it into one paragraph

Combine your answers into a short statement for your website, profile, or offer:

> "We use AI for `___` so we can work faster and at lower cost. A person always takes part in `___` because an error there would be expensive. `___` is responsible for the result. If something goes wrong, email or call `___`, and a person will review it. We do not take on `___`; another provider will be a better fit for that work."

This is your honest AI policy. Update it whenever you change what you hand over to a machine.
