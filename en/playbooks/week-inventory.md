# Worksheet: inventory one week

> Practicum for the task buckets exercise in Chapter 3 and the three-lever filter in Chapter 2.
> Time: one evening. Use your **calendar and messages from last week**, not your job description. Record what you actually did.

**Faster option:** Use the [interactive browser version](https://cheap-intelligence.vercel.app/en/skills/week-inventory/). Enter your tasks and the tool calculates your exposure. You do not need to install anything, and your data stays on your device.

1. List **10 to 15 tasks** that took up your time last week. Be specific. Do not write "managed projects." Write "collected a contractor status update," "wrote to an unhappy customer," or "prepared a table for management."
2. Put each task in a **bucket**: green, "AI alone"; yellow, "human plus AI"; or red, "human only." Judge the task by what today's tools can do, not by habit. If you are unsure, use the two questions from Chapter 3: Is the result digital? Can the work be done remotely?
3. Assign each task a **lever** from Chapter 2: 1, new revenue; 2, cost savings; 3, attention and trust; or "none" if it has no lever. That kind of task keeps you busy but does not create value.
4. Calculate the **share of your time in the green and yellow buckets**. This is your personal exposure. You are measuring your own week, not the profession as a whole.

## Three questions to answer afterward

1. Which of my tasks have **already become cheaper**, even if I still do them by hand?
2. Which tasks do I keep because they make me **feel busy**, rather than because they change a result?
3. Which red-bucket tasks are almost missing from my week, **and why**? Does my role lack them, or am I avoiding them?

## Three honest scenarios

- **Put the list away:** A year from now, your week looks the same, but the market values it less.
- **Go faster inside the green bucket:** This helps as a skill but fails as a strategy. Volume grows where the premium is moving toward zero (Chapter 2).
- **Shift the weight:** Set a six-month target of adding **two or three red-bucket tasks to your week**. This takes longer than learning new prompts, but it moves you toward work whose value is rising.
