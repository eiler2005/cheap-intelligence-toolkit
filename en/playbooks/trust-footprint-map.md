# Trust footprint map: make it easier to choose you

> Practicum for **Book 3, *The Economics of Trust*** (the Creation house). Time: 30 to 40 minutes.
> A worksheet for an independent professional or small business. Fill it in on paper or in a note.

A review, resume, or feed full of "experts" can be faked in an evening. That makes **verifiable human work** more valuable. The person who is easiest to verify can beat the loudest one. This worksheet is not a personal-brand exercise. It reduces the work another person must do to check the claims that matter, even when you are not there to explain them.

> **Use four questions throughout the worksheet.** Is the signal hard to fake? Does it build over time? Can someone check it from outside? Will it lower the cost of another check during a future decision?
> A badge, a self-description, and three glowing reviews fail this test. A long work history, real clients, a person willing to confirm the work, and a channel with a past perform much better. You can make a first move in one evening. It becomes capital through repetition, outside support, and consequences.

## Step 1. Three anchors: what do people check?

Professional trust has three anchors. Mark what you already have that someone can **verify**. "I am good" does not count. "Someone can confirm this without me" does.

| Anchor | What it means | What I have now | Expensive to fake? (yes/no) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Verifiable history** | real work, case studies, dates, and results that someone can check | `__________` | |
| **Live references** | named people who will confirm the work and speak with someone | `__________` | |
| **Signed channel** | a named channel with your history and identity, rather than an anonymous account | `__________` | |

> An empty row is a weak point. One complete anchor marked "expensive to fake: yes" carries more weight than ten bold claims.

The third anchor does not depend on the platform alone. It depends on **consequences**. Words in a signed channel are tied to you, so you may have to answer for them. That is why people trust the channel more than an anonymous review. The statement has a price.

## Step 2. Four channels in your footprint

Leave a trail where people look for you. For each channel, record what already exists, what a stranger can see without you, and one move for the next month. You do not need all four. Choose one or two that your clients really use.

| Channel | What already exists | What people can see without me | One move this month |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Social media / Telegram:** where you think in public | | | |
| **Talks / conferences:** where people saw you live | | | |
| **Books / publications / articles:** what remains in writing | | | |
| **Community:** a smaller circle where people know you personally | | | |

> A smaller circle of people who know you can be stronger than broad reach. Trust is deeper there, and a reputation among real people is hard to fake. This is your "dark forest." See the [Trust compass](trust-compass.md).

### What a machine can read

Some parties checking you are already software systems. A client may ask an assistant instead of a search engine. A machine has no intuition and will not fill in missing context for you.

| What leaves a trail for a person | What a machine can extract |
|---|---|
| A post where you think in public | your specialty, if you use the same wording across sources |
| A talk where people saw you | name, topic, date, and venue, if they appear in text |
| A publication or case study | a figure with a unit, a date, and a link to the primary source |
| A conversation inside a community | nothing, because the closed circle is invisible to the machine |

This does not mean "write for robots." State facts that affect a decision clearly. Name the responsible person instead of "the team." Give a date instead of "recently." Link the primary source instead of saying "research shows." For current context, see [how much of the web is machine traffic](https://cheap-intelligence.vercel.app/en/watch/machine-web).

## Step 3. Keep a show-your-work rhythm

You cannot create authenticity capital by declaring it. It remains as a side effect of real work. Publish an artifact rather than a post about yourself: a case analysis, a method, a record of how you thought, or an honest review of an error. One artifact a week or a month can create a trail over a year that is hard to fake.

- My show-your-work format: `__________________`
- Where I publish it (one channel I control): `__________________`
- A realistic frequency: `__________________`

Rule: **process is more convincing than polish.** "How it worked" and "where I was wrong" often build more trust than a perfect storefront.

## Step 4. Signed channel and provenance

One channel that is clearly yours is a strong anchor. You can also attach **content provenance**, a record of origin and changes, to important material so another person cannot easily claim your work, or put your name on a fake.

- My official channel, where I send everyone: `__________________`
- Where I tell clients that **I write only from this channel** to protect them from impersonation: `__________________`
- Content provenance ([Content Credentials / C2PA](https://contentauthenticity.org/)) is optional. Consider it for photos, videos, or other material that someone could present under a false name. *Verified: 2026-07. Support is growing in cameras and Adobe products, but the label does not always reach the viewer. It is an extra signal, not a guarantee.* Current status and limits appear in the [regulatory tracker](https://cheap-intelligence.vercel.app/en/watch/trust-regulation).

### Personal provenance in one evening

Six settings that help people confirm your authorship without you:

- [ ] **I can find the originals.** Source photos, text versions, and talk recordings stay in one place after publication.
- [ ] **Important material has a date I can show.** I publish it in my channel, export it with a date, or email the attachment to myself.
- [ ] **I have named one official channel** and say so where people most often find me.
- [ ] **I state what I never do.** For example, I do not write first about money, request transfers, or give access through a messenger. People can reject an impersonator without forensic analysis.
- [ ] **I enable content provenance** for material that someone may realistically present under another name.
- [ ] **I know where to go if someone puts my name on their work:** the platform, complaint route, and deadline. Current contacts appear in the [regulatory tracker](https://cheap-intelligence.vercel.app/en/watch/trust-regulation).

> Provenance is not about paranoia or a particular technology. It makes confirmation **cheap** for a person who has doubts about whether the material is yours.

## Step 5. Can AI search see you, and what does it say?

A client may ask an AI system "whom should I hire?" instead of using Google. If you do not appear in the answer, you are absent from that decision. If the answer describes you poorly, you can lose trust before the client reaches your site. Use the [AI search visibility kit](geo-ai-search-kit.md) for a full measurement. For a quick check of the structure an AI can cite:

- [ ] My position or specialty stays consistent over time.
- [ ] I have a clear method that another person can describe.
- [ ] My work has a named author rather than an anonymous label.
- [ ] I have a stable address, channel, or page that does not keep moving.
- [ ] I have a history of accurate work that someone can confirm.

More checked boxes make it easier for both a person and an AI assistant to recommend you.

## Result: one move

Find the empty place that is **cheapest for you to repair and most expensive for your client to check**:

- My weakest anchor: `__________`
- One move this month that will make it verifiable: `__________________`

> Test the move with four questions. Is it hard to fake? Will it build over time? Can someone verify it without you? Will it simplify more than one future check? If yes, you are building authenticity capital. If the move only improves the storefront quickly, it is cosmetic rather than a footprint.
