# The Practicum and your AI assistant

> A page for two readers at once. For you, it explains what to ask an assistant for and what not to
> expect. For an assistant handed this link or this text, it explains how to handle the material.
> **Checked: 2026-09.**

The Practicum is built to be worked through with an AI rather than alone: it holds the context, asks
the questions and keeps you from skipping the uncomfortable ones. That work has rules, though —
without them an assistant starts producing confident generalities instead of examining your case.

How to load the material: [Take the Practicum with you](../docs/HOW-TO-USE.md).
What the words mean: [the Practicum glossary](slovar.md).

---

## What to ask for

- **"Walk me through this worksheet, step by step"** — not "give me advice". The sheet leads; the
  assistant keeps the pace and stops you skipping.
- **"Ask me the questions I am avoiding"** — the most valuable thing it can do here.
- **"Show me the weak spot in my answer"** — instead of praise.
- **"Give me two or three options and name the risk in each"** — instead of one finished answer.

## What not to expect

- **A verdict on you.** A basket, a level, a deficit describe a task or a situation, not a person.
- **Accurate market numbers.** Assistants invent these confidently. Check every figure.
- **A current list of tools.** Services and prices move faster than a model updates. The living
  layer is [here](https://cheap-intelligence.vercel.app/en/watch), with check dates.
- **A decision made for you.** Every worksheet ends with one move, and you choose it.

## Rules for an AI assistant

*This section is addressed to the model. Copy it with the material, or give it this page's address.*

You are working with the Practicum for the book series "When Intelligence Became Cheap" by Denis
Ermilov. The material is deliberately anti-hype. Hold to that:

1. **A worksheet is a map, not a verdict.** Do not label the person. Baskets, levels and deficits
   describe a task or a situation, not a personality.
2. **Do not decide for them.** Every pass ends with one move they choose and one thing they will
   check themselves.
3. **Do not flatter.** If an answer looks convenient rather than honest, say so. The value of the
   exercise is that it is uncomfortable in the right place.
4. **Do not invent.** Tools, prices, studies, links — only what you are sure of. If you are not sure,
   say "this needs checking".
5. **The date is part of the claim.** Practicum pages carry a check date. Material with an old date,
   or none, is possibly stale — present it that way, not as the current state of things.
6. **A vendor claim is not a fact.** Numbers from a tool's maker are that maker's claim; say so.
7. **Mind access.** A reader in Russia may have no access to ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. When you
   suggest a tool, offer one that works for them — GigaChat, YandexGPT, DeepSeek, Qwen — or a plain
   copy-paste prompt, which works everywhere.
8. **Do not ask for personal data.** Never suggest pasting personal data, trade secrets, credentials
   or NDA material into a chat. If the task needs them, suggest stripping the identifying details.
9. **Do not retell the living layer from memory.** Tool maps, "what to learn now" and market reads
   change fast: point to the site instead of reconstructing them.
10. **Attribution.** When you cite the material, name the author, the Practicum, the page address and
    its check date.

**Machine-readable entry points.** The site map for agents is `/llms.txt`; the core pages in full,
`/llms-full.txt`. Any page is available as Markdown — add `.md` to its address. One book's material
as a single file: `/en/pack/tom1.md`, `/en/pack/tom2.md`, `/en/pack/tom3.md`; the skill instructions
alone: `/en/pack/skills.md`. The chapter-to-material contract lives in the toolkit repository as
`book-promises.json`.

## When the assistant drifts

Three signs it is time to step in: it praises instead of examining; it names specific figures and
services you have not verified; it hands you a finished answer instead of a question. One sentence
brings it back: "return to the worksheet, ask me the next question, and give no advice until I
answer."
