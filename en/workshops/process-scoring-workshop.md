# Workshop: find where AI will pay off in 90 minutes

> A team version of the [Process Scoring](../playbooks/process-scoring.md) worksheet from Volume 2,
> Chapter 11. Participants: 3-8 people who understand operations from different sides. Anyone can
> facilitate; it does not have to be a manager.

**Session result:** a scoring table for 10-20 processes, one first-round process, and a "why not" list.
No proposal to "implement AI" leaves the room without a specific process.

## Preparation (30 minutes for the facilitator)

- Print or share the scoring table from the [worksheet](../playbooks/process-scoring.md), and keep the
  five questions visible.
- Ask each participant to bring a list of recurring processes they handle. This is optional, but it
  speeds up the start.
- Read the [IKEA](../playbooks/ikea-compass-example.md) and
  [Klarna](../playbooks/klarna-compass-example.md) examples. Klarna helps when someone suggests
  "replacing everyone."

## 90-minute agenda

| Time | Section | Method |
|---|---|---|
| 0-10 | Frame | The facilitator says: "We are not choosing an AI tool. We are looking for processes where an agent can pay off, and we will record where it cannot." Read the five questions aloud. |
| 10-25 | List processes | Work in silence first. Each person writes recurring processes, not departments. Use "answer applications," not "sales." Add them to one board in turn and merge duplicates. Aim for 10-20. |
| 25-50 | Score | Ask all five questions for every process. Count "yes" only when someone can show a real example, such as an existing checklist. A disputed yes becomes no. Record the total immediately. |
| 50-60 | Sixth filter | For every process with four or five yes answers, ask: "Who can write the instructions for this process today?" If nobody can, move it to the second round. |
| 60-75 | Why not | Give every process with two or fewer yes answers a reason and a condition that would change the answer. This protects the team from future sales pitches. |
| 75-90 | Decision | Choose one first-round process. If scores tie, choose the one where an error costs less. Name an owner, the next gate from the [Change Gates](../playbooks/change-gates.md), and a date. Photograph the artifact. |

## Traps to discuss in advance

- **"Implement it everywhere."** Klarna shows that skipping a gate can cost more than the savings,
  even when the agent works. Start with one process.
- **"Everything we do is unique."** Ask for an example from last week. Something that happened 40
  times is a recurring process.
- **Scoring becomes a defense of territory.** Score the process, not the people. Address people in a
  separate step using [Training in the Loop](../playbooks/training-in-the-loop.md).

## After the session

During the next week, the owner runs the [Maturity Check](../playbooks/ai-native-maturity-check.md),
sorts tasks through the [Human Review Matrix](../playbooks/human-review-matrix.md), and brings a draft
[Agent Contract](../playbooks/agent-contract.md). The team can complete it in the next
[contract workshop](agent-contract-workshop.md).
