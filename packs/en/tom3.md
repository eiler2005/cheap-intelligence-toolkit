# Book 3 Workbook: The Economics of Trust

> The end-to-end Practicum route for *The Economics of Trust* (Book 3 in the
> *When Intelligence Became Cheap* series).
> This route through the contents asks **whom to trust when content, voices, and reputations are cheap
> to fake, and how to become someone people can trust without excessive checking.**
>
> **Verifiability** is the axis of this route. Do not rely on a verbal claim. Arrange your work so that
> important facts about you can be confirmed without your help. For the professional route, use the
> [Volume 1 Workbook](tom1-workbook.md). For the business route, use the
> [Volume 2 Workbook](tom2-workbook.md).

Trust has two sides in this volume. The first is **protection:** avoid a fake call, email, or review,
without paying the cost of checking everything. The second is **creation:** build a verifiable trail
so that people and even AI search can choose you with less doubt. The workbook covers both sides.

> **Did you arrive from a specific chapter?** The Practicum uses three "homes," not one page per
> chapter. The [chapter index](tom3-chapter-index.md) gives each chapter one row and links straight to
> its material.

## Minimum trust package

If you do not have time for the whole volume, complete these five worksheets in one evening:

   human is guaranteed, and who owns an error.

These five worksheets cover the main route through the volume. You have basic protection, the
important file remains intact, and other people can verify and choose you more easily.

## The volume's three homes

### Home 1: Protection (Chapters 1 to 5). Avoid traps without paying to check everything

The diagnosis is simple: convincing no longer means authentic. Voices, faces, reviews, and resumes
can all be faked. You do not need to check everything. Check the cases where an error is costly.

- [Family trust protocol](family-trust-protocol.md) for **home:** a check phrase, a conversation with
  parents and children, common schemes, limits for your own agents, a note by the phone, and the first
  hours after a scam.
- [Personal trust perimeter](trust-perimeter.md) for **work and money:** a second channel for costly
  instructions, a work trail created before an accusation, protection during an accusation or a
  review attack, and your personal "distrust tax" account.
- Live page, for readers in Russia: [protections available there](../watch/ru-fraud-protection.md), including voluntary
  credit bans, the "second hand" rule, cooling-off periods, and the rule that a bank does not write
  through messengers. These measures are free and take minutes to enable.

### Home 2: Verifiability and creation (Chapters 6 to 12). Keep a trail and become easier to verify

Procedure connects protection and trust. Preserve an important file and build your own trail over
time. When fakes are cheap, **verifiable human work** becomes more valuable.

- [Chain of custody: the first 24 hours](chain-of-custody.md) explains what to do with an important
  file before an expert review or dispute: preserve the original, a log, a checksum, and an independent trace.
- [Evidence package before a dispute](evidence-package.md) gives you a template for money, work, a
  deal, or reputation: what happened, where the original is, what the context was, and who can confirm
  it independently. It also asks how much checking is enough for a video, a hire, and a payment.
- Live page: [trust and regulation](../watch/trust-regulation.md) covers who accepts a digital file,
  how to check the origin of someone else's file, and who owns an agent's error.
- [Trust footprint map](trust-footprint-map.md) is the **flagship worksheet.** It puts three anchors,
  a verifiable history, live references, and a signed channel, across four channels: social media or
  Telegram, talks or conferences, books or publications, and community. Its main question is:
  *which parts are expensive to fake?*
- [Honest AI policy](honest-ai-policy.md) treats trust as part of the product. It asks you to state
  how AI is used and make clear who your offer is not for.
- [Trust inside a team](trust-inside-team.md) covers work controls: a register of bots that speak in
  your name and a hiring protocol for a world where an on-screen candidate is cheap to fake.
- [Trust repair protocol](trust-repair-protocol.md) covers the other side of a verifiable trail: what
  to do when you made the mistake. First decide whether competence or honesty failed, then use the
  right process for that failure.
- Live and reusable: the [AI search visibility kit](geo-ai-search-kit.md) helps neural systems find
  and cite you. Its trust layer asks not only whether you are visible, but what those systems say about you.

### Home 3: Compass (Chapter 13). Choose the depth and check both sides

The final chapter turns the material into a decision route. It relies on boring protocols that still
work when you are tired, not heroic vigilance.

- [Trust Compass](trust-compass.md) has one cost-of-error arrow, three levels of depth, two territories
  (Home and Work and business), two sides (how I check and what I show), and four norms.
- [Family trust protocol](family-trust-protocol.md) is a standalone route for a broad audience. It
  covers family members, parents, children, your own agents, and the first hours after a scam.
- Live page: the [regulatory tracker](../watch/trust-regulation.md) covers AI labeling, provenance,
  and laws. It changes quickly.

# Family trust protocol: one conversation before the urgent call

> Practicum for **Book 3, *The Economics of Trust*, Chapter 13**.
> Setup time: 15 to 20 minutes. This protocol does not teach you to detect a fake voice.
> It gives you steps that work whether the voice is real or fake.

## 1. Choose a family check phrase

The phrase is not a password that authorizes a payment. It is a **stop signal**. A correct answer lowers the risk, but you still confirm an expensive request through an independent channel.

A good phrase:

- does not appear on social media, in messages, or in family greetings;
- is not a pet's name, address, birth date, or mother's maiden name;
- is easy to say and remember;
- is known only to relatives who follow the agreement.

We keep our phrase in this agreed secure place, not on this page: _______________

People who know it: ___________________________________________________________

Test it aloud once during a normal conversation. Do not send it through the same chat where a fraudulent request could arrive.

## 2. Agree on the response

If a voice, video, or message from a relative asks for money, access, a document, or a secret code:

1. Ask for the check phrase.
2. Whatever the answer, do not pay or share information through the same contact.
3. End the conversation.
4. Contact your relative yourself through a saved number or another relative.
5. Do nothing if you cannot confirm the request.

Urgency does not stop you from helping. It stops you from acting through one channel. Treat anyone who claims to represent a bank or public agency the same way: end the call, then contact the organization through an official number you found yourself.

### What these scams usually look like

You do not need a catalog of horrors or a lesson in detecting a fake by ear. That approach does not work. These schemes follow a similar script, and one rule can break all of them.

| Scheme | What happens | What makes the attack harder |
|---|---|---|
| "A relative is in trouble" | A voice that sounds like a child or grandchild urgently asks for money | Ask for the check phrase, then call the saved number yourself |
| "Bank security" | The caller leads you toward a transfer to a "safe account" or asks for a text-message code | Hang up and call the number printed on the card |
| A call presented as a survey | The caller tries to collect a recording of your voice to build a clone | Do not have long conversations with unknown callers |
| A video message "from a relative" | The face and voice look real, but the request comes from someone else | A video is not confirmation. Apply the same rule |

> Shared pattern: **urgency plus money, data, or access means stop.** A real emergency can survive a two-minute check.

Workplace schemes, including a fake message from an executive, changed payment details, and review attacks, appear in the [Personal trust perimeter](trust-perimeter.md).

## 3. Talk with parents and older relatives

Do not start with a list of technologies or frightening examples. Say this:

> "Scammers can now speak with the voices of people you know. You do not have to tell a real voice from a fake one. If anyone asks for money, hang up and call me yourself using your saved number. The real me will never be offended by the check."

Then add the second half:

> "If they fool you, or if you only start to feel unsure, call me right away. I will not scold you or take away your independence. First we will stop the loss. Then we will work out what happened."

Removing shame is part of the protection. A person who fears the family's reaction waits longer before calling the bank or police.

### Add the bank controls available where you live

Check whether your bank lets you lower transfer limits, require an extra confirmation for large payments, freeze a card quickly, or disable features you do not use. Names and rules differ by bank and country. Use the bank's official app or site to confirm the current terms, then choose controls your family can explain and use.

Do not assume that a cooling-off period, dispute deadline, or reimbursement rule works the same way everywhere. Check the current process with your bank or local financial regulator before you need it.

## 4. Children and teenagers

A child needs the same check phrase and a shorter rule:

> "If an adult you do not know says your parents are in trouble, asks you to turn on the camera, find money, show valuables, or keep the call secret, end the conversation and call your trusted adult right away."

A child does not help an "investigation" by phone, show the home on video, or search for family money at a caller's direction.

## 5. Your AI agents and family money

If an assistant can order or pay for things, make an agreement with it too:

| Setting | Our decision |
|---|---|
| Maximum amount for one purchase | |
| Maximum amount per month | |
| What it may buy on its own | |
| What it may only add to a cart | |
| Which subscriptions and extra charges are forbidden | |
| Where we can see notifications and the activity log | |
| How to cancel an action or dispute a payment | |

If you cannot remember the agent's limit, assume there is no limit.

## 6. The first hours after fraud

Stop the loss first. Work out how it happened afterward.

1. **Contact the bank through an official number** printed on the back of the card, shown in the banking app, or listed on the bank's official site. If you may have exposed card details or remote access, ask the bank to block the card and remote access.
2. **Preserve the trail:** time, phone numbers, addresses, messages, receipts, screenshots, and the amount. Do not delete or "improve" anything.
3. **Ask the bank to open a dispute or recall request** if money moved without your consent or because of fraud. Follow the deadline and evidence rules in your country. Record the case number and every answer.
4. **Use your local fraud-reporting route.** This may be the police, a national fraud service, or a financial regulator. Keep the report number and any written confirmation.
5. **Tell someone close to you.** Do not face the shame and follow-up calls alone. So-called helpers may promise to recover the money and try to scam you again.

A bank does not always have to return the money, especially when the customer confirmed the transfer. Do not promise yourself or your relative a guaranteed refund. Act quickly and record every contact.

## Card to keep by the phone

> **ARE THEY ASKING FOR MONEY OR DATA?**
>
> 1. I do not argue or transfer anything.
> 2. I hang up.
> 3. I call my relative or bank myself using a saved official number.
> 4. I never share text-message codes, card details, or passwords.
> 5. If I already shared or transferred something, I call the bank and my relative at once.
>
> **A real relative will not be offended by a check.**

Print this block in large type and leave it near the phone. Rehearse the sequence once: who calls whom, which number they use, and what happens if the first person does not answer.

## A separate large-print card for older relatives

Print this version in the largest practical type and leave it by your parents' home phone. It is shorter because nobody reads a long list during a stressful call.

> **OUR PHRASE: `______________`**
>
> If someone calls and asks for money, even in the voice of my child or grandchild:
>
> 1. I ask for our phrase.
> 2. I hang up. This is not rude.
> 3. I call back myself using my saved number.
> 4. I never share a text-message code. With anyone. Ever.
> 5. A bank or the police will never ask me to transfer money to a "safe account."
>
> **If I already transferred money or shared a code, I call right away:**
>
> My relative: `______________` | Bank: number on the back of my card
>
> **Nobody will scold me. The sooner I call, the better the chance of getting the money back.**

The last line is required. Shame can keep a person silent for a full day, and that day can decide whether the money comes back.

# Personal trust perimeter: work, money, and your good name

> Practicum for **Book 3, *The Economics of Trust*** (the Protection house). Setup time: 20 minutes.
> Four protocols for the working side of life. Set up Sections 1 and 2 in advance. Read Sections 3 and 4 when you need them.

These protocols are not about paranoia. They make deception **more expensive and easier to notice**, and they help you stay calm. When an email "from the CEO" arrives, or someone claims that "AI wrote this," a rule you created in advance matters more than alertness. Set it up once. It will still work when you are tired or in a hurry.

> **Home has its own worksheet.** The [Family trust protocol](family-trust-protocol.md) contains the check phrase, conversations with parents and children, common call scams, and a card to keep by the phone. Start there. It protects family money, while this worksheet protects work and reputation.

---

## 1. A second channel for expensive instructions at work

A convincing message "from management" is a new wrapper around an old fraud. A procedure protects you better than your eye.

1. **List expensive actions** that nobody may approve from one call or message, however convincing it looks. Examples include a payment, changed payment details, access transfer, or data release. Your list: `__________`
2. **Set a second-channel rule.** If an instruction arrives by voice, video, or messenger, confirm it through an independent channel in the company directory. Do not use contact details from the incoming message.
3. **Give the manager a script for the team:** "I will never ask you to make a transfer through a messenger. If I do, call my work number. That is an instruction."
4. **Give junior employees the right to verify.** A callback follows procedure. It does not show distrust, and nobody is punished for the pause.

---

## 2. Build a work trail before an accusation

AI detectors can accuse a real person of using a model. Protect the work in advance with one setting that can serve you for years.

- [ ] I create important work in a system that **keeps version history**, such as Google Docs, Microsoft 365, Notion, or a Git repository.
- [ ] I keep drafts and source material. A writer keeps versions, an analyst keeps data and calculations, a photographer keeps source files, and a developer keeps commits.
- [ ] I establish dates at no cost by emailing a draft to myself. For important work, I also export a dated copy.

### If someone accuses you

Do not defend yourself with words alone. **Show the process.** Edit history is more convincing than a final file or a detector score. Use this order:

1. **Do not argue about the percentage.** The detector score is neither something you can debate nor the claim you need to disprove. Move the conversation to the process.
2. **Show how the work developed:** version history, dated drafts, intermediate versions, source files, and data. One screen with edit history can resolve the question faster than an hour of explanation.
3. **Answer questions about the substance.** The author can explain any part without reading from the text: why they chose an approach, where a figure came from, and what they removed.
4. **Ask for the basis of the decision in writing.** If the only basis is a percentage, preserve that fact. The next question is about the decision process, not your character.
5. **Do not accept an oral compromise** when the outcome matters, including a grade, disciplinary action, or termination. Ask for the decision and its basis in writing.

**There is support for this approach in Russian court practice.** In June 2026, the Supreme Court of Tatarstan ruled that a student's expulsion over alleged "AI text" in a thesis was unlawful. Three consecutive checks of the same file in Antiplagiat.VUZ reported AI-content scores ranging from 41.91% to 11.92%. The court stated that the system's report **was probabilistic**, while the final decision belonged to an expert. The student was reinstated, and the university had to pay compensation for moral harm and a fine.
Here, "probabilistic" means that the score expressed likelihood, not proof. Source: *Kommersant*, June 8, 2026:
[the court's position](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8727298) and
[reinstatement and compensation](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8727878). *Verified: 2026-07-29.*

Practical result: ask for another check of the same file and record the spread. The same text producing unstable scores became an argument the court understood.

### If you are the decision-maker

This is the other side of the same problem, for a teacher, manager, or editor. Before making a decision based on a "percentage," ask:

- [ ] Do I understand **what the detector measured** and know its error rate?
- [ ] Did I ask to see the process rather than demand a defense?
- [ ] Did I ask at least one question about the substance of the work?
- [ ] Am I punishing a style, such as careful prose, a formulaic genre, or writing by a nonnative speaker?
- [ ] Do I have a **second independent** signal beyond the score?
- [ ] Did I let the person respond **before** the decision rather than after it?

> The cost of an error may be another person's reputation, diploma, or job. In this case, verify your own decision as carefully as you verify their text.

---

## 3. Review attack: the first 24 hours for a small business

If someone floods your business with fake reviews or demands money to remove them:

1. **Do not pay.** Payment does not stop the attack and tells the attacker that you may pay again.
2. **Preserve the evidence.** Save screenshots of reviews, profiles, and demands, with dates and times.
3. **Report it to the platform** through its fake-review or extortion channel. Current forms appear in the [regulatory tracker](../watch/trust-regulation.md).
4. **Ask real customers for help.** Publish an honest note and ask for genuine reviews. Do not buy a batch of positive reviews as a "repair." That continues the same downward path.
5. **Report extortion to the police.** Bring in a lawyer if the scale calls for one.

---

## 4. Your personal distrust-tax account

This optional exercise takes one week. Choose **one** process, such as incoming calls, order intake, or a family phone. Track what distrust costs:

| Line | What to record |
|---|---|
| Verification | minutes and money spent checking |
| Delay | what waited while the check happened |
| False rejection | which real person or request you rejected as fake |
| Missed fake | what still got through |

Question for the week: **Where did I verify something strange when I should have verified something expensive?** This calibrates your effort toward decisions with a high cost of error instead of every unusual detail.

### The mirror: what do you cost other people?

There is another side to the account. You do not only pay a distrust tax. You may charge it to a client, customer, colleague, or employer. Review the same four lines from their point of view:

| Line | What to inspect about yourself |
|---|---|
| Verification | how many steps someone takes before believing they can work with you |
| Delay | how long they wait for you to confirm something personally |
| False rejection | how many good people leave before they get confirmation |
| Accepted on trust | which claims in your offer cannot be checked at all |

One question: **What claim about me cannot be confirmed without asking me?** Each answer is a tax other people pay and a reason to choose someone else.

- Most expensive line: `__________`
- First thing to make verifiable: `__________`

Take that answer into the [Trust footprint map](trust-footprint-map.md), where it becomes one move for the month.

# Trust footprint map: make it easier to choose you

> Practicum for **Book 3, *The Economics of Trust*** (the Creation house). Time: 30 to 40 minutes.
> A worksheet for an independent professional or small business. Fill it in on paper or in a note.

A review, resume, or feed full of "experts" can be faked in an evening. That makes **verifiable human work** more valuable. The person who is easiest to verify can beat the loudest one. This worksheet is not a personal-brand exercise. It reduces the work another person must do to check the claims that matter, even when you are not there to explain them.

> **Use four questions throughout the worksheet.** Is the signal hard to fake? Does it build over time? Can someone check it from outside? Will it lower the cost of another check during a future decision?
> A badge, a self-description, and three glowing reviews fail this test. A long work history, real clients, a person willing to confirm the work, and a channel with a past perform much better. You can make a first move in one evening. It becomes capital through repetition, outside support, and consequences.

---

## Step 1. Three anchors: what do people check?

Professional trust has three anchors. Mark what you already have that someone can **verify**. "I am good" does not count. "Someone can confirm this without me" does.

| Anchor | What it means | What I have now | Expensive to fake? (yes/no) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Verifiable history** | real work, case studies, dates, and results that someone can check | `__________` | |
| **Live references** | named people who will confirm the work and speak with someone | `__________` | |
| **Signed channel** | a named channel with your history and identity, rather than an anonymous account | `__________` | |

> An empty row is a weak point. One complete anchor marked "expensive to fake: yes" carries more weight than ten bold claims.

The third anchor does not depend on the platform alone. It depends on **consequences**. Words in a signed channel are tied to you, so you may have to answer for them. That is why people trust the channel more than an anonymous review. The statement has a price.

---

## Step 2. Four channels in your footprint

Leave a trail where people look for you. For each channel, record what already exists, what a stranger can see without you, and one move for the next month. You do not need all four. Choose one or two that your clients really use.

| Channel | What already exists | What people can see without me | One move this month |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Social media / Telegram:** where you think in public | | | |
| **Talks / conferences:** where people saw you live | | | |
| **Books / publications / articles:** what remains in writing | | | |
| **Community:** a smaller circle where people know you personally | | | |

> A smaller circle of people who know you can be stronger than broad reach. Trust is deeper there, and a reputation among real people is hard to fake. This is your "dark forest." See the [Trust compass](trust-compass.md).

### What a machine can read

Some parties checking you are already software systems. A client may ask an assistant instead of a search engine. A machine has no intuition and will not fill in missing context for you.

| What leaves a trail for a person | What a machine can extract |
|---|---|
| A post where you think in public | your specialty, if you use the same wording across sources |
| A talk where people saw you | name, topic, date, and venue, if they appear in text |
| A publication or case study | a figure with a unit, a date, and a link to the primary source |
| A conversation inside a community | nothing, because the closed circle is invisible to the machine |

This does not mean "write for robots." State facts that affect a decision clearly. Name the responsible person instead of "the team." Give a date instead of "recently." Link the primary source instead of saying "research shows." For current context, see [how much of the web is machine traffic](../watch/machine-web.md).

---

## Step 3. Keep a show-your-work rhythm

You cannot create authenticity capital by declaring it. It remains as a side effect of real work. Publish an artifact rather than a post about yourself: a case analysis, a method, a record of how you thought, or an honest review of an error. One artifact a week or a month can create a trail over a year that is hard to fake.

- My show-your-work format: `__________________`
- Where I publish it (one channel I control): `__________________`
- A realistic frequency: `__________________`

Rule: **process is more convincing than polish.** "How it worked" and "where I was wrong" often build more trust than a perfect storefront.

---

## Step 4. Signed channel and provenance

One channel that is clearly yours is a strong anchor. You can also attach **content provenance**, a record of origin and changes, to important material so another person cannot easily claim your work, or put your name on a fake.

- My official channel, where I send everyone: `__________________`
- Where I tell clients that **I write only from this channel** to protect them from impersonation: `__________________`
- Content provenance ([Content Credentials / C2PA](https://contentauthenticity.org/)) is optional. Consider it for photos, videos, or other material that someone could present under a false name. *Verified: 2026-07. Support is growing in cameras and Adobe products, but the label does not always reach the viewer. It is an extra signal, not a guarantee.* Current status and limits appear in the [regulatory tracker](../watch/trust-regulation.md).

### Personal provenance in one evening

- [ ] **I know where to go if someone puts my name on their work:** the platform, complaint route, and deadline. Current contacts appear in the [regulatory tracker](../watch/trust-regulation.md).

> Practicum for **Volume 3, Chapters 6 and 10**. Time: 20 minutes for one situation.

|---|---|---|
| **Event** | date, participants, action, amount, or subject | |
| **Source** | original file, email, contract, photo, or recording | |
| **Context** | messages before and after, document version, call record | |
| **Independent record** | statement, system log, email header, or confirmation from another person | |

## 5. How much is enough for a video, a hire, or a payment?

A formal dispute is the extreme case. You usually need to answer a smaller question: **How much verification is enough for this decision?** The answer depends on the cost of being wrong, not on how strange the item looks.

### What a credential really confirms

| Credential or evidence | What it confirms | What it does not confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Diploma or certificate | the person completed a program at a stated time | that they can do the work today |
| Platform review | someone posted positive words | that the author exists or had the claimed experience |
| Case study or portfolio | a result is shown | that this person produced it |
| Product demo | the system works on prepared data | that it will work on your data |
| Named recommendation | the author is willing to put their reputation behind the statement | that they examined the work closely |
| Independent audit or measurement | a third party checked something with a stated method | that the method fits your situation |
| Signed file or provenance record | origin and change history | that the content is true |

### How much to check at different levels of risk

| Decision | What you lose if you are wrong | Enough verification |
|---|---|---|
| Watch or forward something funny | nothing | none; save your attention |
| Cite something publicly as fact | your reputation | primary source and date |
| Hire someone | months of work and access | live conversation, work artifacts, and one independent person who will stand behind the reference |
| Pay or change payment details | money, often with no way back | confirmation through a channel known **in advance**; for a large amount, add a second signer or limit |
| Share data or access | the change may be irreversible and may affect other people | all of the above, plus a written basis and an expiration date |

Record the result as one of three states: **confirmed, probable, or unknown**. Apply the state to this decision, not forever. Strong verification does not pretend to settle everything. It makes the remaining uncertainty visible.

# Chain of custody: the first day with an important file

> Practicum for **Volume 3, Chapter 10**. Time: 10 to 15 minutes.
> This is a practical first-preservation protocol, not digital forensics or legal advice.

An important file rarely loses a dispute because it looks unconvincing. The problem is often simpler. Nobody knows where the file came from, whether it changed, or who handled it. This worksheet helps you preserve your options. It cannot guarantee that a bank, newsroom, or court will accept the file, but it can stop you from destroying a useful trail yourself.

---

## Start with four actions

### 1. Save the original

- Take the source file from the device or service where it first appeared.
- Do not make a messenger copy your only copy. The service may compress the file or remove some metadata.
- Create a working copy. Store the original separately and do not edit it again.
- Preserve the nearby context: the conversation, email, call record, receipt, and login log.

**Path to the original:** ________________________________________________

### 2. Record who, when, and what

| Date and time | Who had the file | What they did | Recipient or storage location |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | |
| | | | |
| | | | |

Fill in the log right away. You can add to your own log later, so the log alone proves nothing. Its value comes from links to independent records, such as an email, cloud version history, bank case number, or police report number.

### 3. Create an independent record of the file

Choose a method that matches the cost of a possible error:

- A **checksum** shows that two files match bit for bit. It does not identify the author or prove a date by itself.
- A **channel with an external timestamp**, such as a company system, cloud version history, or registered case, can help show that the file already existed at a certain time.
- A **specialist or notary** is useful when a dispute is already likely and the cost of an error is high, not simply "just in case."

**Checksum or external record:** __________________________________________

### 4. Do not improve the original

Noise reduction, upscaling, editing, and a command such as "make it clearer" create a derivative version. That version may be easier to inspect, but it is no longer identical to what the device recorded.

- Leave the original untouched.
- Give an enhanced or cropped version a different name.
- Record which tool created the derivative copy and why.

---

## During the first day

- [ ] Contact the bank on the same day if money is involved. Save the case number.
- [ ] Save the bank statement, receipt, and recipient details.
- [ ] Export the conversation or email with dates and technical headers when they are available.
- [ ] Record the names of people who witnessed the event or saw the file.
- [ ] Ask the employer, platform, or contractor to preserve system logs if they control them.
- [ ] If a dispute is likely, contact the right party: the bank and police for money or fraud, a lawyer for a contract or workplace issue, or a fact-checking desk for a publication.

## What usually weakens a file

- the only copy is a forwarded message or screenshot;
- renaming the file without recording its original name;
- editing the original;
- deleting nearby messages and notifications;
- waiting a long time before creating the first independent record;
- assuming that "the recording speaks for itself."

---

## Transfer record

Copy this block every time you transfer the file:

```text
File:
Original name:
When and where it appeared:
Received from:
Checksum:
Transferred to:
Date and method of transfer:
What was transferred: original / exact copy / derivative copy
Changes made:
Related independent record:
```

This protocol is based on [NIST SP 800-86](https://www.nist.gov/publications/guide-integrating-forensic-techniques-incident-response): record every action involving digital material, preserve the source, and verify integrity. NIST describes it as a practical guide to computer forensics, not legal instructions for every country.

> The useful result is not "I verify more." It is **"I verify somewhere else."**
> The compass makes deception more expensive. It does not make anyone invulnerable.

# The trust compass (Practicum for "When Intelligence Became Cheap")

📋 A ready-made skill for an AI assistant. Drop this file into your agent's skills folder — or copy
the block below into any chat.

The compass does not detect fakes and does not decide whether a person, voice, image or file is
real. It helps you name **what checking you decide in advance is enough** when being wrong is
expensive. The worksheet this comes from, for filling in by hand:
[The trust compass](../../playbooks/trust-compass.md).

```text
Help me build a trust protocol using the trust compass from "The Economics of Trust" (chapter 13).

The core rule: the depth of checking is set by the cost of being wrong, not by how convincing the
message is. The other side controls how convincing it looks. I control the cost of being wrong.

Three depths:
— No questions: the mistake is small and reversible (a clip, an image, someone's opinion). Do not
  check; save the attention for what is expensive.
— One question: I am about to cite it, forward it or form a view on it. Ask "where did this come
  from?" and find the source.
— All three questions plus independent confirmation: money, access, documents, health, work,
  reputation. Check the origin, the speaker and the accountability — and step onto a second channel
  the sender does not control.

Collect from me if I have not given it:
1. My three expensive scenarios: one at home, one at work, one of any kind.
2. Who usually gets in touch in them, and through which channels.

What to do:
1. Assign a depth to each scenario and justify it by the cost of being wrong, not by how convincing
   the approach looks.
2. For the deepest scenarios, name a specific second channel the sender does not control. Not
   "call the number in the message" but "call the number I already had".
3. Help me separate three states honestly: confirmed · probable · unknown. "Probable" is not "yes";
   spell out what I did not check.
4. Propose one household agreement and one work agreement — short enough to say out loud and
   remember in a hurry.
5. Finish with ONE move for the coming week.

Discipline:
- Do not rule that something "is fake" or "is genuine": you have no grounds for that.
- Do not tell me to check everything. Attention is finite, which is the whole point of the compass.
- Do not offer AI-detection services as proof.
- Say separately what I will present when it is me being checked.

Answer format:
1. Table: scenario | cost of being wrong | depth | what I actually do.
2. The second channel for the expensive scenarios.
3. The honesty line: confirmed / probable / unknown.
4. Two agreements: home and work.
5. One move for the week.
```
